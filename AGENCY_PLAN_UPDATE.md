# CiteBrief Agency Plan Update

Date: 14 Sep 2026  
Status: recommended product and pricing update

## Executive Summary

Yes: at $249/month, CiteBrief cannot feel like a thin AI visibility tracker. For an agency, $249 must feel like a business tool that creates a new recurring client deliverable, saves account-manager time, helps retain clients, and opens upsell conversations.

The product should be updated from:

> AI visibility tracking for agencies.

To:

> The weekly AI-search reporting system agencies use to prove value, protect retainers, and sell the next piece of work.

The key change is that CiteBrief should not only show where a client appears in AI answers. It should package that data into a client-ready story with recommended actions and revenue opportunities for the agency.

## Core Positioning

### Primary Position

> The Friday AI-search report agencies send to clients.

### Stronger Revenue Position

> CiteBrief turns ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, and Google AI Overview checks into a white-label client report with competitor wins, source evidence, and recommended next actions.

### What The Product Really Sells

CiteBrief does not sell software access. It sells a repeatable agency deliverable.

The agency should be able to say to clients:

> Every week, we check whether AI search is recommending you or your competitors, and we send you a clear action plan.

That makes CiteBrief part of the agency's retainer, not just another reporting expense.

## Why $249 Needs More Value

At $249/month, an agency buyer will ask:

- Does this save my team time?
- Can my account managers send this to clients without rewriting it?
- Can I use this across enough client accounts?
- Does this help me retain a client?
- Does this help me upsell GEO, SEO, content, PR, or conversion work?
- Does the output look premium enough to represent my agency?

If the answer is only "it tracks AI visibility," then $249 feels expensive.

If the answer is "it gives you a weekly white-label report for up to 10 clients and tells your team what to sell next," then $249 becomes easy to justify.

## Updated Product Thesis

CiteBrief should be built around three layers:

1. Measurement: where the client appears across AI answer engines.
2. Explanation: what changed, who won, and why it matters.
3. Monetization: what the agency should do next and how to explain it to the client.

The third layer is what makes the product worth $249/month.

## Required Product Updates

### 1. Client-Ready Friday Summary

Every report should start with a short plain-English summary:

- What changed this week
- Where the client was visible
- Where competitors won
- What this means for the client's business
- What the agency recommends next

This summary is the part account managers will forward, screenshot, or paste into client emails.

### 2. Recommended Agency Actions

Each report should include 3-5 recommended actions.

Examples:

- Update the comparison page because competitors are winning shortlist queries.
- Add pricing proof because AI answers mention uncertainty around cost.
- Publish an alternatives page because competitors are cited more often.
- Improve homepage positioning because the client is named but not recommended.
- Create source-worthy content around the buyer question that failed.

The product should make the next agency task obvious.

### 3. Upsell Opportunity Layer

Each report should identify monetizable opportunities:

- Content refresh
- Comparison page
- PR/source placement
- Technical SEO cleanup
- Landing page update
- Review/site authority work
- New GEO retainer package

This is critical. Agencies pay more when the product helps them sell more.

### 4. Client Risk Flags

CiteBrief should flag clients that need attention:

- Visibility dropped sharply
- Competitor overtook them
- Client is named but not recommended
- AI answers cite weak or outdated sources
- Client has no presence for key buyer questions

This helps agency owners protect retainers before the client complains.

### 5. Report Email Composer

The app should generate a suggested email message for each report.

It should include:

- Short subject line
- 3-bullet client summary
- Link to the report
- Suggested next step

The agency should be able to edit before sending.

### 6. Report Approval Flow

For agencies, reports should not always send automatically.

Recommended workflow:

1. Report is generated.
2. Account manager reviews it.
3. Account manager edits the email summary or notes.
4. Report is approved.
5. Report is sent to the client.

This keeps the product useful while protecting agency-client relationships.

### 7. Agency Dashboard

The Agency plan should include a simple portfolio dashboard:

- Total client brands
- Reports due this week
- Reports sent
- Clients with visibility drops
- Clients with upsell opportunities
- Failed or partial report runs

This makes the product feel operational, not just analytical.
